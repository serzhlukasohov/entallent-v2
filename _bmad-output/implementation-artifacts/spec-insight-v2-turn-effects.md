# Durable conversation turn effects

## Goal

Recover a failed or evicted TypeScript conversation job without applying its survey verdict twice, creating a second outbound response, or leaving an eligible V2 reply behind the cutoff barrier. This story covers the active conversation worker and its PostgreSQL/Redis adapters. It does not decide whether a Slack event first persisted after cutoff is eligible; apply the separately approved ingress policy when it arrives.

## Current boundary

`ConversationOrchestrator.orchestrate` changes durable state before `ConversationProcessor` writes its receipt. In order, the path can enqueue profile hydration, apply a Bundle or clarification verdict, write risk/follow-up decisions, update the active topic, save an outbound message, stage a confirmation prompt, and enqueue delivery, memory, style, and survey-evidence jobs. BullMQ retry re-enters the entire method. A stable delivery job ID protects one saved outbound ID, but another orchestration can create another outbound ID. The existing receipt-only retry is safe only after the whole orchestrator has returned.

Migrated-PostgreSQL/Redis proof: when `enqueueMessageSend` queues the first response and then raises, the two-attempt conversation job runs again. The fixture observes two distinct delivered outbound IDs and one conversation receipt for the same inbound. This is an observed duplicate delivery, not only a theoretical failure window.

Mitigation in progress: the outbound ID is now derived from the persisted inbound ID, and repository replay accepts only matching scope and response text. The same two-attempt fixture now observes one delivered outbound. This does not make the verdict-to-outbound boundary atomic, recover a missing queue intent, or make conflicting regenerated content recoverable. Tasks 2–5 remain required.

Database foundation (2026-09-29): migration `0039` adds an immutable, scoped inbound-to-outbound turn record and identifier-only dispatch intents for message send, memory, style, survey evidence, profile hydration, and follow-up execution. PostgreSQL guards reject a wrong response source, wrong person, wrong dispatch target, or follow-up action from another inbound. The migrated integration fixture verifies these constraints and cascade cleanup. The active worker writes the turn record and enabled send, memory, style, and evidence intents; profile hydration and follow-up execution are not yet wired.

Group-report intent scope (2026-09-29): migration `0040` adds `group_report` with the confirmed group-state ID as its target. The guard requires the state to belong to this turn's employee and inbound confirmation, and its window to point at a matching reporting cohort and roster. The migrated fixture covers rejection of an arbitrary target and acceptance of the scoped confirmed state. The active V1 report enqueue still bypasses the intent ledger until turn commit is wired.

Committed response wiring (2026-09-29): the live conversation worker now locks a scoped inbound admission within the turn transaction, records one outbound effect and content-free intents for response send, memory extraction, style analysis, and survey evidence when enabled. Each intent is marked after BullMQ accepts a stable-ID job. A retry reads the committed effect and dispatches only unqueued intents without rerunning model interpretation. A concurrent BullMQ case exposed duplicate survey evidence dispatch and now proves one stable job for that effect. Migrated PostgreSQL/BullMQ also covers a forged employee scope, failure before outbound persistence, and failure after delivery enqueue. Recurring Redis-loss reconciliation and earlier risk/reminder/Pulse backlog writes remain open; an uncertain Slack send must not be replayed automatically without a delivery-attempt boundary.

Committed-turn recovery (2026-09-29): migration `0041` records one immutable send attempt before the channel call. The cutoff scanner can recover a committed turn whose original Redis job failed or disappeared and can queue a send intent that has no attempt. The queued admission selector retains a timely reply after Bundle purge only when the same tenant, employee, and conversation have a committed turn effect. A migrated PostgreSQL/Redis failure case proves one verdict, one outbound, one send attempt, one receipt, and eventual cleanup after the original job fails before send enqueue. An attempted Slack send with uncertain outcome remains manual reconciliation; profile, follow-up, and group-report intents remain open.

Profile and non-send recovery (2026-09-29): profile hydration is now an identifier-only committed intent, dispatched after the turn commits with a stable inbound-derived Redis job ID. A response-model failure leaves no profile job. The recurring scanner also finds old unqueued intents for committed turns outside a survey window and queues scoped conversation recovery; a migrated PostgreSQL/Redis case proves one recovered profile job without repeating model work. This covers enqueue failure before an intent is marked queued. Lost Redis jobs after that mark still require a durable completion or reconciliation rule for non-send effects. Follow-up and group-report intents remain open.

Already-queued non-send boundary (2026-09-29): `last_queued_at` records BullMQ acceptance, not execution. The scanner only selects null `last_queued_at`; a missing Redis job may have completed and been removed, or may have been lost before work. Requeueing by absence alone would repeat memory, style, evidence, profile, follow-up, or report side effects. The next implementation must add a scoped durable completion/result receipt for each kind, written by its consumer, and recover only aged intents without that receipt after validating the domain result and job state. Keep message-send's separate attempt boundary; its uncertain Slack outcome cannot use this rule.

Recovery design check (2026-09-29): profile hydration's `profileHydration.lastAttemptAt` is best-effort telemetry; a successful profile update can commit even when this timestamp fails to persist, so it cannot serve as the completion receipt. Follow-up execution saves and queues a new outbound before marking its action `sent`, which similarly leaves a crash window where a replay could generate another outbound. The durable completion rule must be coupled to each consumer's committed domain effect, with a separate unknown-outcome state wherever an external send may already have happened. A Redis job's absence or a stale `last_queued_at` alone is insufficient proof that replay is safe.

Profile recovery implementation (2026-09-29): forward migrations `0044` and `0045` add an immutable completion timestamp to dispatch intents and protect its transition. The active profile worker carries the inbound ID to a scoped repository operation that locks the intent and commits the profile facts, operational outcome, and completion timestamp in one PostgreSQL transaction. It skips external lookup when already complete. The recurring scanner selects aged queued profile intents with no completion receipt, checks the original Redis job state, and enqueues one identifier-only recovery job when the original is terminal or missing. A migrated PostgreSQL plus real Redis Worker fixture proves rollback on outcome failure, recovered execution, one profile update/outcome, and removal from the recovery selector. Other non-send kinds remain open because their side effects need their own atomic result boundaries.

Style recovery implementation (2026-09-29): committed style-analysis jobs now read conversation history only through their inbound source, so delayed execution cannot analyze future employee messages. The style profile and completion receipt commit in one scoped PostgreSQL transaction; insufficient source-bounded turns complete with no model call or profile write. The recurring scanner restores an aged queued style job only when the original Redis job is terminal or absent. A migrated PostgreSQL/Redis Worker fixture proves an unqueued completion failure rolls back its profile, then lost-job recovery applies the style update once and excludes a later private message. Memory, evidence, follow-up, and group-report intents still need equivalent result boundaries.

Employee reminder transaction (2026-09-29): the reminder is parsed before response generation but the scheduled action is saved only inside the committed turn transaction. Its source-scoped `follow_up_execution` intent names the action ID. Redis delivery is delayed until after commit and uses a stable action-and-due-time job ID. A migrated PostgreSQL/Redis test forces a failure before turn-effect persistence, proves the reminder and outbound both roll back, then retries and observes one scheduled action, one intent, and one delayed job. The cutoff scanner can resume an old unqueued follow-up intent from a committed turn. User-level concurrent deduplication and already-queued Redis job loss remain open.

Shared transaction boundary (2026-09-29): the worker `DatabaseService` exposes `withTransaction`, routing repository calls in the current asynchronous context through one PostgreSQL transaction. A migrated integration test proves rollback, isolation of parallel turns, and a nested repository transaction rolling back to a savepoint without aborting the outer turn. The orchestrator uses this boundary for prepared V2 verdicts, outbound response, prompt staging, and the committed response record. Earlier risk, reminder, and Pulse backlog effects still need preparation before a full atomic turn is achieved.

V2 verdict preparation (2026-09-29): Bundle and clarification interpretations now remain proposals while the conversation response is generated. Read-only repository previews select the next disputed question for a same-turn clarification prompt; application only applies a non-unrelated verdict after successful response preparation. Model-failure regressions prove no question verdict or outbound is written, and the migrated PostgreSQL/Redis flow passes all 18 scenarios. Risk, reminder, V1 confirmation, outbound staging, and queue effects remain outside one shared commit and must move into the proposed-turn boundary before task 4 is complete.

Preparation audit: `findSurveyProbe` calls `PulseBacklogService.getNextProbeQuestion`, which may create a window, initialize and reprioritize backlog, or unlock Engagement before the reply exists. Task 3 must split that selection from these writes as well; moving only the explicit repository saves below response generation would leave a pre-commit side effect.

## Tasks

Assert the current duplicate or stranded behavior first. Cover two concurrent executions for the same inbound ID and retained/evicted BullMQ history.
2. **Turn ownership and durable plan.** Extend `packages/database/src/schema/conversation-job-admissions.ts` with a scoped committed-effect identity, and add a forward migration and integration test under `packages/database/src/__tests__/`. A committed record must identify exactly one inbound and outbound row under the same tenant, person, and conversation. Keep private response text in the outbound `messages` row. Add identifier-only dispatch intents with stable keys; reject arbitrary text payloads.
3. **Prepare before effects.** In `packages/application/src/use-cases/conversation-orchestrator.ts`, resolve AI classification, V2 interpretation, response, and validation into a proposed turn before any question verdict, risk/follow-up write, active-topic write, outbound save, prompt staging, or queue dispatch. Enrichment jobs must also become intents. Continue to validate inbound ownership before model access. Expose the proposed effects through application ports rather than SQL or BullMQ types.
4. **Single PostgreSQL commit.** In worker adapters, lock the scoped admission and atomically apply the proposed verdict and other TypeScript-owned state, save one outbound row, stage any V2 or V1 prompt, record the committed effect, and insert dispatch intents. Repository methods currently opening their own transactions must accept the shared transaction boundary. On a concurrent retry, return the already committed outbound identity and intents without applying the proposal again. A failed transaction must leave the admission uncommitted and all proposed effects absent.
5. **Dispatch and reconciliation.** Dispatch committed intents after the database transaction with stable BullMQ job IDs. Retry missing dispatches from PostgreSQL even after Redis loss; mark each dispatched intent only after successful enqueue. Replace blind failed-job retry and completed-job-only receipt recovery in `apps/worker/src/conversation/conversation.processor.ts` and the cutoff scanner with a scoped reconciliation operation: committed turns resume dispatch/receipt; uncommitted turns may rerun preparation; invalid ownership or irrecoverable conflicts require a content-free terminal record and alert. Keep the private Bundle hold until receipt or reviewed terminal resolution.
6. **Acceptance and runbook.** Prove one verdict, one outbound, one delivered response, one receipt, and eventual private-content purge across the failure matrix. Verify no private response or confirmed meaning appears in Redis payloads, intent rows, errors, or logs. Update `docs/insight-analysis-v2-cutoff-recovery.md`, the requirement tracker, and the task log. Run targeted unit/integration tests, typecheck, and `pnpm harness:check -- --base origin/main` with named PostgreSQL/Redis targets.

## Acceptance criteria

- Given a model or database failure before commit, when the same inbound is retried, then at most one transaction commits and exactly one outbound identity is produced.
- Given a crash after any committed effect or Redis enqueue, when reconciliation runs, then it dispatches only the missing identifier-only intents and records one receipt without repeating interpretation or delivery.
- Given an in-period V2 clarification whose worker finishes after cutoff, when the committed turn is reconciled, then the verdict is applied once and temporary content is purged after the receipt.
- Given an unauthorized or mismatched inbound scope, when preparation or reconciliation runs, then no model call, domain write, or dispatch occurs.
- Given any failed or evicted original BullMQ job, when the recurring scanner runs, then it uses the durable turn state rather than assuming success or blindly rerunning the old job.
