# Onboarding v2 — answers to developer questions

Decision record: 2026-10-07. Language: English; decisions agreed in the Russian-language product discussion.

Scope: requirements and answers only. This document does not implement, deploy, or verify runtime behavior. Developer descriptions of existing code in OPEN-QUESTIONS.md are context supplied by developers, not independently verified facts.

This document supplements docs/onboarding-v2.md. Later decisions below take precedence where they differ. Every original question ID is retained; the source contains no Q7. Each section includes the original question and decision request, followed by the agreed answer.

Key updates: Slack buttons plus clear text equivalents; substantive engagement starts participation and analysis; a user-initiated return resumes participation after refusal; Team Lead Got It enables Pulse participation; reminder has no buttons; Manager/HR/HRBP/Leadership receive no proactive conversations or reminders; privacy and retention are separate tasks.

## Q2. Can typed text count as pressing a button?

**Original developer question:** Can typed text count as pressing a button?

**Original decision request:** Confirm option 2, and whether Emma should confirm before applying ("Got it, I'll check back on Wednesday, OK?") or apply silently.

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

All proposed Next Actions are actual Slack buttons, not expected reply phrases. Buttons must be offered in the initial onboarding message. A clearly equivalent text reply may perform the same action, including starting participation. Interpret meaning in context rather than relying only on exact phrase matches. If intent is ambiguous, Emma asks for clarification and does not change participation until it is clear. Apply an unambiguous choice and acknowledge it; an additional confirmation click is not required. Onboarding actions never approve use of insights in reports.

## Q2a. Does "ok, thanks" from a Manager count as Got It?

**Original developer question:** Does "ok, thanks" from a Manager count as Got It?

**Original decision request:** Can Got It be inferred from an acknowledging reply, or must it be the button?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Yes, an acknowledging reply such as “ok, thanks” can count as Got It when it clearly acknowledges the introduction in context. If the reply contains uncertainty or an unresolved question, Emma responds and clarifies where needed. A button click is not mandatory. Manager, HR, HRBP, and Leadership do not receive onboarding reminders (Q14).

## Q2b. Does telling Emma about a problem count as Let's Talk?

**Original developer question:** Does telling Emma about a problem count as Let's Talk?

**Original decision request:** Is starting a real conversation the same as agreeing to be invited regularly?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Yes. If an Employee starts a substantive conversation instead of clicking, onboarding is complete and future proactive conversations are enabled. Emma responds to the user's topic immediately. The conversation is immediately analyzed as a source of insights for backlog questions. This is participation and analysis, not permission to use the resulting insights in team reports or management recommendations; that approval remains separate. Team Leads also participate in Pulse Check (Q10). A greeting or an onboarding clarification alone does not count as substantive engagement (Q15).

## Q3. What does Emma do with messages from people who have not opted in?

**Original developer question:** What does Emma do with messages from people who have not opted in?

**Original decision request:** Which of the four processes run for undecided people, and which for people who declined? Are those two groups treated the same?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

An explicit refusal, by button or clearly equivalent text, stops proactive conversations and reminders indefinitely. Emma does not contact the employee again until the employee initiates a new conversation, even if a year passes. A user-initiated return automatically resumes participation, analysis for the backlog, and future proactive conversations; no repeat onboarding or separate re-enrolment confirmation is needed. For an undecided employee, a substantive conversation completes onboarding and starts analysis (Q2b). Reporting approval is still separate.

The agreed decision concerns participation, proactive contact, and backlog analysis. It does not introduce new risk-detection or memory policies. Existing private conversational support must not be represented as authorization to disclose individual data. Additional rules for risk processing, memory retention, or special follow-ups are not newly specified in this onboarding clarification.

## Q4. How does an active participant opt out later?

**Original developer question:** How does an active participant opt out later?

**Original decision request:** What is the supported way to stop check-ins after joining?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

A clear textual request to stop participation immediately stops proactive conversations and reminders. Emma acknowledges the decision without requiring a confirmation button. If the employee may mean only ending today's conversation, Emma clarifies instead of treating that as a permanent refusal. A later user-initiated conversation resumes participation under Q3/Q5.

## Q5. How does someone who declined come back?

**Original developer question:** How does someone who declined come back?

**Original decision request:** When, if ever, does Emma offer rejoining to someone who declined?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

The employee returns simply by initiating a new conversation. Emma responds without repeating onboarding. Participation, backlog analysis, and future proactive conversations resume automatically, even after a long absence. An old Let's Talk button can also express a return (Q6). Emma does not proactively solicit a return while the refusal remains in force. Insight use in reports still requires separate approval.

## Q6. What happens when an old button is clicked?

**Original developer question:** What happens when an old button is clicked?

**Original decision request:** Which old clicks count as a change of mind?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

| Old interaction | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Later after participation has started | Do not change participation. Briefly explain that the introduction is already complete. |
| Let's Talk after refusal | Resume participation and begin the conversation. |
| I Don't Want to Participate after participation has started | Stop proactive conversations and acknowledge refusal. |
| Duplicate click on the same action | Do not duplicate the conversation, reminder, or acknowledgement. |

These rules cover delayed/stale interactions. Accepted choices normally remove the buttons (Q24), and reminders have no buttons (Q14).

## Q8. What happens when someone is promoted or demoted?

**Original developer question:** What happens when someone is promoted or demoted?

**Original decision request:** Does a role change trigger the new role's intro? Does personal participation survive a promotion to Manager, and if not, are their check-ins stopped?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

| Role change | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Employee → Team Lead | Show a short introduction to the new management capabilities with Got It. Preserve the existing personal participation decision; Team Lead remains a Pulse participant. |
| Team Lead → Manager | Show the management introduction. Stop personal Pulse invitations and collection of new Pulse insights because Managers are not Pulse participants in the MVP. |
| Manager → Employee | Show personal onboarding and offer participation again without enabling it automatically. |
| Other role changes | Explain newly relevant capabilities and access boundaries without unnecessarily repeating familiar sections. |

Role changes do not grant approval for insight use. Personal support remains available on user initiative (Q11).

## Q9. What happens on deactivation and reactivation?

**Original developer question:** What happens on deactivation and reactivation?

**Original decision request:** Does a returning person start onboarding from scratch?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Deactivation stops all messages and cancels pending invitations/reminders. Reactivation of the same person preserves completed onboarding and the last participation decision. An active participant resumes proactive conversations without receiving accumulated invitations. A person who declined receives no proactive contact until they initiate a conversation. Unfinished onboarding can continue while preserving its one-reminder limit. If the role changed, apply Q8 as well.

## Q10. Is a Team Lead who pressed Got It ever invited personally?

**Original developer question:** Is a Team Lead who pressed Got It ever invited personally?

**Original decision request:** Is that intended, or should there be one later personal invitation for Team Leads who only pressed Got It?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Yes. Team Lead is a Pulse Check participant, and Emma talks with them about backlog topics and may invite them proactively. Got It completes the introduction without starting a conversation immediately; it does not exclude the Team Lead from Pulse participation. Explicit refusal stops invitations under the same rules as for Employees. This replaces the earlier statement that Got It implied no personal opt-in and left the Team Lead indefinitely undecided.

## Q11. Can Managers, HR, HRBP, Leadership have personal conversations?

**Original developer question:** Can Managers, HR, HRBP, Leadership have personal conversations?

**Original decision request:** For these four roles: may they use Emma for personal support? Do they ever receive proactive check-ins? Is anything they say ever Pulse evidence?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Manager, HR, HRBP, and Leadership may initiate conversations for personal support or management situations. Emma does not proactively start conversations with these roles. They are not Pulse Check participants in the MVP, and their conversations are not analyzed as sources for the Pulse backlog. The initial onboarding messages still exist; there are no subsequent onboarding reminders for these roles (Q14).

## Q12. When exactly is "after two working days"?

**Original developer question:** When exactly is "after two working days"?

**Original decision request:** A precise rule, ideally with two or three worked examples that become test cases.

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Count the next two working dates in the company's configured timezone and calendar, retaining the local time of the originating event. If that time is outside working hours, use the next available working time. For silence, count from onboarding delivery; for Later, count from its button click or accepted textual equivalent.

For Monday–Friday, 09:00–18:00:

| Origin | Reminder |
| --- | --- |
| Monday 15:00 delivery, unanswered | Wednesday 15:00 |
| Thursday 17:55 Later | Monday 17:55 |
| Saturday 12:00 delivery, unanswered | Tuesday 12:00; Monday and Tuesday are the next two working dates. |

The origin date does not count as one of the next two working dates.

## Q13. Does a second Later move the reminder?

**Original developer question:** Does a second Later move the reminder?

**Original decision request:** Yes, reschedule; or no, keep the first date.

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Yes. A later Later choice reschedules an unsent reminder to two working days after the latest choice. Monday Later followed by Tuesday Later moves Wednesday's reminder to Thursday at the relevant local time. The budget remains one reminder per onboarding. Once that reminder is sent, further Later choices do not create another.

## Q14. What does the reminder look like?

**Original developer question:** What does the reminder look like?

**Original decision request:** Does it repeat the full intro or just a short nudge? Does it carry the same buttons as the intro?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Send a short reminder, not the full introduction. No buttons are included. Text replies follow Q2. Send at most one reminder.

Employee:

> Hi! Just checking in — would you like to talk? You can share what's on your mind, or we can start with how things are going at work. No pressure — you can come back anytime. I won't send another onboarding reminder.

Team Lead:

> Hi! Would you like to finish our introduction? We can start a conversation about how things are going for you at work, or you can acknowledge the introduction for now. I won't send another onboarding reminder.

No reminders are sent to Manager, HR, HRBP, or Leadership. This narrows the earlier any-role reminder requirement.

## Q15. Does chatting count as "silence"?

**Original developer question:** Does chatting count as "silence"?

**Original decision request:** Should any conversation cancel or postpone the reminder?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

A substantive conversation completes onboarding and cancels the onboarding reminder, even without a click. A greeting or an onboarding clarification alone does not complete it: Emma answers, and the reminder stays scheduled. Do not interrupt a substantive ongoing conversation with an onboarding reminder.

## Q16. Calendar changes and public holidays

**Original developer question:** Calendar changes and public holidays

**Original decision request:** Recalculate scheduled reminders when the calendar changes? Ignore public holidays for MVP?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Recalculate unsent reminders using the current company calendar, from the original triggering event or latest Later choice, following the two-working-day rule. If the revised due date has passed, send at the next working time. Public holidays are not automatically included in the MVP; use the configured weekdays and hours. Calendar recalculation neither creates an additional reminder nor overrides refusal.

## Q20. Can the customer see which named employee declined?

**Original developer question:** Can the customer see which named employee declined?

**Original decision request:** Who may see individual onboarding decisions?

**Status:** Decided

**Agreed answer:**

Customers cannot see named employees' participation choices: acceptance, deferral, or refusal. They may see technical onboarding delivery statuses and aggregate counts without names, protected against identifying individuals in small groups. Only enTalent's team may see individual onboarding decisions for operational support. This is not permission to read conversations or individual answers; client-release privacy is covered by Q21.

## Q21. Is closing internal access to conversations part of onboarding?

**Original developer question:** Is closing internal access to conversations part of onboarding?

**Original decision request:** Where does this work belong, and does onboarding wait for it?

**Status:** Separate workstream / outside onboarding scope

**Agreed answer:**

Keep internal access to conversations open during MVP development so the team can test easily. Removing that access is a separate task, not part of onboarding implementation. Before client launch, conversations must become inaccessible to everyone except the user themselves. Prepare the onboarding messages now with the planned privacy promise; do not weaken or rewrite them to describe temporary testing access. Onboarding development can proceed in parallel with the access-control task.
